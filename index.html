Why a hand-engraved chart

Three arguments for ink

Read how a plate is made →

01 · The line

A burin leaves what a plotter cannot

Engraved lines swell and thin with the pressure of a wrist that surveyed the shore it draws. On a printed chart that variation is noise. On a wall it is the record of a morning.

02 · The paper

Paper that ages like a coast does

We print on cotton wove and clamp-leaved stock that yellows the way tide-stained wood does, evenly and slowly. The plate is the negative; your wall does the developing.

03 · The record

Every plate carries its doubts

Where the survey was uncertain, the plate says so, in margin notes cut by the surveyor. A chart that admits its gaps ages better than one that pretends to certainty.

Bespoke work

A chart of water you know

Most commissions begin the same way: someone sends us a stretch of coast they have sailed, swum or grown up beside, and asks for it drawn as it deserves.

We take four bespoke plates a season. The survey takes as long as it takes; the engraving, between nine and fourteen weeks. You get the plate's first eight impressions and the right of refusal on the working proofs.
